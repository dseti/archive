## What is dreamwork?

Dreamwork is the practice of working with dreams.  Typically, it involves a process, tradition, guide, facilitator, priest, therapist, etc.  You naturally do dreamwork when you share your dream with a partner, ask about its meaning, and wonder if you should take some action, like talking to the person you dreamed about. There are several definitions of that are particularly relevant for D-SETI Dreamwork.

First, D-SETI Dreamwork looks to the ancient Greek practice of dream incubation that is expressed through the traditions related to Asclepius, the god of dreams and medicine.  These traditions represent a mystical and shamanic legacy from which Western science arose, but then ignored.  For example, I understand the Socratic Myths to be archetypal examples of Western dreamwork.

Second, D-SETI looks to the definition of hypnotic dreams, especially through Deidre Barrett's dissertation.  Barrett used hypnosis to induce dream-like trances, suggesting to the hypnotee that they have a dream.  She then compared the content of the reports.  To my eyes, this use of hypnosis is the most pure and archetypal use of hypnosis, from which the various models of regression hypnosis unconsciously derive. 

Third, D-SETI looks to contemporary dreamwork traditions that derive from Kilton Stewart's influence, which intersected with academic dreamwork but caused controversy.  Stewart, like Carlos Castenda, wrote a fiction about another culture regarding real imaginal things.  This definition of dreamwork opens D-SETI up to the cultural practice of working with dreams, regardless if the literary basis derives from fiction.

There are many other influences on D-SETI Dreamwork, which include Carl Jung and Michael Harner.  D-SETI looks to the International Association of the Study of Dreams for ethical guidelines in dreamwork practice and education.
