Mack describes how abductees often slip into altered, trance-like states with minimal prompting. A simple relaxation exercise allows memories to surface in symbolic fragments. These fragments include floating sensations, luminous rooms, and the presence of beings surrounding the experiencer. The state resembles the threshold between wakefulness and dreaming, containing drifting images and emotional surges. Memories appear more like dream symbols than historical scenes, yet carry the weight of lived experience. Mack emphasizes that this hybrid state is particularly useful for exploring encounters, for it reveals the dreamlike texture underlying the abduction phenomenon.
