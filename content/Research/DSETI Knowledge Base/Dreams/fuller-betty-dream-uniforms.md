In Fuller’s The Interrupted Journey, Betty Hill recounts the first of her multi-night dreams following the sighting. She dreams of men standing in the road as the car stops, their uniforms neat and emotionless. In the dream, she and Barney are guided wordlessly toward the woods. The scene unfolds with surreal clarity and an atmosphere of controlled inevitability. Betty senses both danger and calm, as if the dream is delivering a message rather than reflecting fear alone. The figures feel human yet uncanny, behaving with rigid ceremonial precision. Fuller presents this dream as the beginning of a larger dream sequence that shaped the Hills’ understanding of their experience and contains symbolic elements of the later hypnosis narratives.
