# Congruence testing frames from beginning, middle, and end of footage

If we are dealing with geometric communication, I expect that the communication will be repeated or obvious in some way. If we are dealing with accidental formation of flares, I expect that the formation will shift over time. The Phoenix Lights remained steady over about 4 minutes of continuous filming. The longest video of the Mojave Triangle is around half a minute.

The steadiness of the formation may be tested and measured through a very simple technique I called “congruence testing”. You take a frame from the beginning, middle, and end of the footage then overlay them on each other. If everything lines up, they’re congruent. If not, then something moved. I used screenshots of videos, then import them into GIMP and use only the move and rotate transforms to align the images. In this test, I used different colors for each frame to visually see the congruence.

To my eyes, all videos except video #2 expressed congruence across their frames. When I compared a frame from the 1st second and 24th second of video #2, I observed that the right bottom-most orb was displaced downward and left one orb length.
