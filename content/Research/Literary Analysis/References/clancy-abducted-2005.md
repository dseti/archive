Abducted by Susan Clancy is a cognitive psychology investigation into how people develop vivid memories of alien abductions. Drawing on interviews, memory research, and experimental data, Clancy argues that sleep paralysis, suggestion, and cultural narratives shape many abduction memories. DSETI tracks this reference because Rekshan cites Clancy in discussions of fantasy proneness, memory distortion, and trauma-like reactions to abduction beliefs. Clancy’s work helps differentiate between literal encounters and interpretations shaped by psychological mechanisms. For DSETI, Abducted provides a scientific framework for understanding how dreamlike or hypnagogic experiences can become integrated as autobiographical memory. It supports ethical and non-pathologizing approaches.
